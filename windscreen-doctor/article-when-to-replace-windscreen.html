When does a windscreen need replacing in South Africa?

Most damaged windscreens that reach a mobile technician are replacement jobs. A chip is often how that starts. A crack, edge damage or shattered glass is how it ends. This guide covers the conditions that typically mean new glass — not a second service sitting next to replacement.

A stone chip

A single small chip can sit for days, then run after a pothole, a slammed door or a hot afternoon. Windscreen Doctor currently specialises in replacement, so a chip is assessed as a reason to fit new glass — especially if it is already spreading, sitting in the driver’s view, or close to the edge.

Read what to do immediately after a chip so the crack does not travel while you wait for an appointment.

Multiple chips

Several impact points on one windscreen are common on South African gravel, mine roads and long N-route stretches. Multiple chips usually mean the original glass is no longer the sensible option. Replacement restores a single, intact screen rather than leaving a constellation of weak points.

A spreading crack

Once a crack has length, it rarely stays put. Vibration, temperature and body flex keep it moving. A long or branching crack is a replacement condition. Do not wait for it to reach the A-pillar.

Damage in the driver’s view

South Africa is right-hand drive. Damage in the driver’s critical view affects safe visibility even if the rest of the glass looks fine. That is typically a replacement call, not something to “live with until month-end”.

Edge damage

Chips or cracks that reach the windscreen edge can compromise how the glass sits in the body. Edge damage is one of the clearer replacement indicators. Send a photo that shows the chip and the edge of the glass.

Shattered or starred glass

If the screen is starred across, spider-webbed or collapsed, the vehicle should not stay in service. Replacement is the job. If the vehicle is not drivable, say so on the quote form so the team can plan around a site visit.

What we need for a quote

Year, make, model, the town or site where the vehicle is standing, and a clear photo. Cameras or sensors on the glass should be mentioned. Some vehicles need additional procedures after replacement; we advise where that applies and do not currently claim to perform ADAS calibration in-house.
